# RegisterDimension: a Lean 4 check of *The Dimension of Space from the Granularity of Hilbert Space* (v16-34)

This project machine-checks the mathematics of the paper: what one ring of `L` cells gives, the derivation of `d = 3` from the two postulates in Secs. II and IV, the areas and pixel counts of the sphere in `d` dimensions, the relaxed count of Sec. VI with its root, and every number the paper quotes, for every `L` between `10⁶¹` and `10⁶²`. The postulates are never axioms here. Each enters as a definition or as a named hypothesis in the statement of the theorem that uses it, so a theorem's statement is its own ledger. For all 102 main theorems, `#print axioms` returns nothing beyond the logical minimum of classical mathematics, `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`. There is no `sorry` and no `native_decide`.

The paper version checked is v16-34 (`dimension_v16-34.tex`); each Lean file names it in its header. Build: Lean `v4.35.0-rc2`, Mathlib commit `045acef0f761280401116e3801cb78ed1b2e716b`. With [elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan) installed, run `lake exe cache get` in this folder, then `lake build`; `lake env lean RegisterDimension/Ledger.lean` prints the ledger. The workflow in `.github/workflows/ci.yml` does both on GitHub after every change, and it fails on any warning and on any main theorem that depends on an axiom beyond the three.

## What each file proves

`Ring.lean` formalizes Secs. II–III on functions on one ring, `ℤ → ℂ` repeating after `L` cells, with Postulate 1 read as the shift `X|j⟩ = |j + 1⟩`, that is `(Xf)(j) = f(j − 1)` on amplitudes. Born's rule is the half-angle identity: with the mean outcome `cos θ = 2m/L − 1`, the frequency of the outcome 1 is `cos²(θ/2) = m/L` (`born`); one notch is the phase step `2π/L` (`phase_succ`). The mode with `κ` wavelengths around the ring is a function on the ring (`mode_periodic`), moving one cell on multiplies it by `e^{2πiκ/L}` (`shift_mode`), it is an eigenfunction of `X` with eigenvalue `e^{−2πiκ/L}` (`shift_eigen`), the `L` roots are distinct (`root_injective`), so momentum along a ring takes exactly `L` values, and a mode is found at every cell with probability `1/L` (`norm_mode`, `mode_uniform`). Roots multiply as their numbers add (`root_add`, `joint_mode`), and the clock and the shift obey `ZX = e^{2πi/L}XZ` (`weyl`). The doublet theorems (`doublet_branches`, `doublet_mean`) check Sec. III's quoted Dirac form; the count does not use them.

`Offsets.lean` proves facts about sums of differences along paths (`pathSum_coboundary`, `closed_sum_invariant`, `ring_offsets`, and the rest). Since v16-34 the paper no longer uses offsets; the file is kept as general mathematics and the count does not use it.

`Vertex.lean` formalizes the count of Sec. IV. Two definitions carry the paper's reading into Lean: the readings of one leg's momentum in a span of dimension `s` are a magnitude with `L` values and `s − 1` angles with `L` values each (`alphabet`), and the cells of the sphere are `L` values in each of `d − 1` coordinates (`cells`). These are labels; no grid is placed on a round sphere. The `L` values per coordinate are the rationality condition of Postulate 1: each coordinate answers a yes/no question about a direction, a two-outcome question is a qubit, and its probability is a multiple of `1/L`. So a leg has `L^s` momenta (`alphabet_card`) and the sphere has `L^(d−1)` cells (`cells_card`, Eq. (cells)); `L^s = L^(d−1)` gives `s = d − 1` (`span_of_postulate`). Conserved momenta, `n + 1` of them summing to zero, span at most `n` dimensions (`finrank_span_le_of_sum_zero`), exactly `n` when the first `n` are independent (`finrank_span_eq_of_sum_zero`), so a vertex whose momenta obey no relation beyond conservation has `d = n + 1` (`dimension_eq_legs`, Eq. (nk)). Counting the orientation of the plane as well would break the count (`orientation_not_counted`), the coincidence of two uncorrelated strings at one cell is one pair in `L` (`coincidence`), and a law that commutes with the shift preserves the mode number (`eigenspace_preserved`).

`Conservation.lean` formalizes the setting of Sec. II and the conservation of Sec. IV from Postulate 1. The register records a string's place only by its phase on each ring through its cell, and no information is lost, so the record is one-to-one; a shift along a ring advances the phase on that ring by one notch and leaves the others. From these, the shifts through a cell commute (`shifts_commute`); `turn_comm` is the model in which a place is its record. Momentum generates translation, so a state of definite momentum is an eigenvector of the shift along every ring through the cell; the ring closes after `L` cells, so each eigenvalue is `e^{2πiκ_u/L}` with `κ_u` whole (`eigenvalue_root`), and every tuple of whole numbers occurs (`productMode_shift`): a momentum is `d` whole-number components. No law depends on the global phase, and moving its origin one cell on is the joint shift, so the vertex intertwines the joint shifts along every ring; a joint mode is an eigenvector of the joint shift with eigenvalue `e^{−2πiΣκ/L}` (`jointMode_shift`), equal roots of unity are equal numbers modulo `L` (`root_eq_iff`), and the total mode number is conserved modulo `L` (`total_mode_conserved`, Noether on ℤ_L). `vertex_exists` constructs such a vertex, so these hypotheses can be met. A ring has one bit per cell, so every ring holds a string at all times; an empty ring holds a uniform string, which the shift leaves unchanged, so it carries no momentum (`uniform_mode_zero`). Momentum is therefore carried only by strings with a pattern, and a quantum that leaves one string can only enter another: when no other string is at the cell, it enters the uniform string of an empty ring. That is emission, and absorption is the reverse; nothing is created. Below the Planck momentum no sum wraps (`eq_of_window`), so for the three-leg vertex `κ_in = κ_out,1 + κ_out,2` along every ring (`three_leg_conservation`), and the momenta sum to zero as vectors (`sum_zero_of_components`).

`Derivation.lean` joins the files. The register's least interaction (`Emission`) is a string passing one notch to a new string, the light, on an empty ring through the same cell: three legs, the string before, the light, and the string after. Coordinates are chosen so that the string's ring and the light's ring are two of the rings through the cell, since the orientation of the rings is a choice of coordinates. A ring carries one string, so the light is not on the string's ring and the string after is not on the light's; an interaction exchanges momentum, so the light's mode number is not zero. With conservation, the three momenta span a plane (`Emission.finrank_legs`); on the way, the string before is shown to move, since at rest the string after would share the light's ring. With Postulate 2, `d = 3` (`three_from_postulates`). `model` is such an interaction at `L = 5` and `d = 3`, and `model_consistent` shows that it meets Postulate 2, so all the hypotheses can be met together.

`Planes.lean` proves that two different planes through the center meet the unit sphere in at most two opposite points (`two_planes_meet`), so two rings in different planes share at most two cells and the coincidence rate `2/L²` of Sec. IV is an upper bound.

`Sphere.lean` formalizes Secs. V–VII. On the two-sphere the fraction of the area within polar angle `θ` of the pole is `u₁ = (1 − cos θ)/2` (`u_two_sphere`), `cos²(θ/2) = 1 − u₁` (`cos_sq_half_eq`), and the `L` values of `u₁` cut the sphere into bands of equal area (`band_area`). The area of the unit sphere is `2π`, `4π`, `2π²`, `8π²/3` at `d = 2, 3, 4, 5` (`Ω_two` to `Ω_five`), the pixel count is `L`, `L²/π`, `L³/4π`, `L⁴/6π²` (`Npix_two` to `Npix_five`, Table I), and the ratio `C(d)` is `1`, `1/π`, `1/4π` (`C_two`, `C_three`, `C_four`). A pixel is a square of one cell, `ℓ²`. A cell of the grid on the horizon has area `ℓ²/π` (`cell_area`), and a pixel subtends one notch, `2π/L` (`pixel_subtends_notch`).

`Root.lean` formalizes Sec. VI, the relaxed count `g(d) = (d − 3) + ln C(d)/ln L = 0`, from the convexity of `ln Γ` (Bohr–Mollerup) with `Γ(1) = Γ(2) = 1`, `Γ(1/2) = √π` and `Γ(x + 1) = xΓ(x)`; no derivative is taken. On `[2, 4]`, `1/4π ≤ C(d) ≤ 1` and `C` decreases (`logC_ge`, `logC_nonpos`, `logC_strictAnti`); `g(2) = −1` and `g(4) > 0` for `L > 4π` (`g_two`, `g_four`), so a root exists (`root_exists`); any root satisfies `0 ≤ d − 3 ≤ ln 4π/ln L` (`root_bound`); `g` increases on `(0, L²/2π)` (`g_strictMono`), so the root is the only solution there (`unique_root`); the chords of `ln Γ` at `3/2` give `ln π/(ln L − ln π) ≤ d − 3 ≤ ln π/(ln L − ln 4)` (`root_bracket`); another pixel moves the root by about `ln c/ln L` (`pixel_shift`); and the factors that would put the root at `7/2` and `5/2` are `√L · C(7/2)` and `√L/C(5/2)` (`seven_halves_factor`, `five_halves_factor`).

`Numerics.lean` certifies every number the paper quotes, for every `L` between `10⁶¹` and `10⁶²`: `ln π` and `ln 10` from `e^x < 1/(1 − x)` and `1 + x ≤ e^x` (`log_pi_bounds`, `log_ten_bounds`), `140.422 < ln L < 142.7984` (`log_L_range`), the leading term `3.008`, the bound `0.018`, the shift `0.010` (`leading_term_range`, `bound_value_range`, `pixel_choice_range`), the root between `3.0080` and `3.0083` and unique below `L²/2π > 10¹²¹` (`root_range`, `uniqueness_range`), the shift for pixels of four cells between `0.0097` and `0.0100` (`pixel_shift_range`), the factor for `7/2` between `5 × 10²⁹` and `2 × 10³⁰` and for `5/2` above `4 × 10³⁰` (`seven_halves_range`, `five_halves_range`), `⌊log₂ L⌋` between `202` and `205` (`capacity_range`), and `1/π = 0.318`, `π = 3.14` (`inv_pi_val`, `pi_val`).

`Ledger.lean` prints the axioms of every main theorem.

## What `d = 3` rests on

`three_from_postulates` takes an `Emission` and Postulate 2, and nothing else.

| Field or hypothesis | What it says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `huv`, `hafter` | the light is not on the string's ring; the string after is not on the light's ring | Postulate 1: a ring carries one string |
| `hκl` | the light's mode number is not zero | the definition of an interaction: momentum is exchanged |
| `hA`, `hmap`, `hc` | along every ring the vertex intertwines the joint shifts and takes the incoming mode to the outgoing one | Postulate 1: no law depends on the global phase; the legs have definite momenta |
| `hwin` | the momenta lie below the Planck momentum | the scope of the theorem: every observable momentum |
| `hpost` | one cell per momentum, as the equality of the two counts | Postulate 2 |

Three things are definitions rather than hypotheses. A ring's phase belongs to the ring, so a shift along one ring leaves the phases on the others (`shifts_commute`). A string's momentum lies along its ring, and coordinates are chosen along the string's ring and the light's. The `L` values per coordinate of `alphabet` and `cells` are the rationality condition of Postulate 1. Quantum mechanics is taken with its Hilbert space, in which a law is a linear map.

The theorem is about the register's least interaction, the one with these three legs. That the least interaction has this form, and that every record is made with it, is argued in Sec. IV of the paper and is the subject of the theorem, not something it proves.

What Lean cannot check is that these definitions and hypotheses are the right model of the register: a proof checker confirms what follows from them, not that they match the world. Everything that follows is checked.

## What is not certified

That the least interaction is a string passing one notch to a new string (Sec. IV, prose); that every record is made with it; that a plane's orientation is `2(d − 2)` angles, of which `orientation_not_counted` checks the arithmetic; the value `1/C = 3.1745` at the root; the second root beyond `L²/2π`; the equal-area partition of the sphere for `d > 3`, which the paper takes from the literature and the files check only for the two-sphere; the remark on the digamma function, replaced by the slopes of `ln Γ`; the two energy branches of the doublet used in the remark on the factor of two; Sec. IX, the Clifford reading; and the citations of Niven and Clifford.
