# RegisterDimension: a Lean 4 check of *The Dimension of Space from the Granularity of Hilbert Space* (v16-29)

This project machine-checks the mathematics of the paper: what one ring of `L` cells gives, the count of Sec. IV, which gives `d = 3` for an event with three legs, the areas and pixel counts of the sphere in `d` dimensions, the relaxed count of Sec. VI with its root, and every number the paper quotes. The postulates and the constants the paper takes from outside are never axioms here. Each is a named hypothesis in the statement of the theorem that uses it, or an exact rational in the file of numbers, so a theorem's statement is its own ledger. For all 95 main theorems, `#print axioms` returns nothing beyond the logical minimum of classical mathematics, `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`. There is no `sorry` and no `native_decide`.

The paper version checked is v16-29 (`dimension_v16-29.tex`); each Lean file names it in its header. Build: Lean `v4.35.0-rc2`, Mathlib commit `045acef0f761280401116e3801cb78ed1b2e716b`. With [elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan) installed, run `lake exe cache get` in this folder to download Mathlib's compiled files, then `lake build`, which compiles every module without errors or warnings; `lake env lean RegisterDimension/Ledger.lean` prints the ledger. The workflow in `.github/workflows/ci.yml` does both on GitHub after every change, and it fails on any warning and on any main theorem that depends on an axiom beyond the three.

## What each file proves

`Ring.lean` formalizes Secs. II–III on functions on one ring, `ℤ → ℂ` repeating after `L` cells, with Postulate 1 read as the shift `X|j⟩ = |j + 1⟩`, that is `(Xf)(j) = f(j − 1)` on amplitudes, and the ring closing after `L` cells. Born's rule is the half-angle identity: with the mean outcome `cos θ = 2m/L − 1`, the frequency of the outcome 1 is `cos²(θ/2) = m/L` (`born`, Eq. (raqm)); one notch is the phase step `2π/L` (`phase_succ`). The mode with `κ` wavelengths around the ring is a function on the ring (`mode_periodic`), moving one cell on multiplies it by the `L`th root of unity `e^{2πiκ/L}` (`shift_mode`), and it is an eigenfunction of `X` with eigenvalue `e^{−2πiκ/L}` (`shift_eigen`); the roots for `κ = 0, …, L − 1` are distinct (`root_injective`), so momentum along a ring takes exactly `L` values, and a mode has the same modulus at every cell, so it is found there with probability `1/L` (`norm_mode`, `mode_uniform`). Roots multiply as their numbers add (`root_add`), so the joint shift of two strings multiplies the product of their modes by `e^{2πi(κ + κ')/L}` (`joint_mode`). The clock and the shift obey `ZX = e^{2πi/L}XZ`, the finite Weyl pair (`weyl`, Sec. X). The Dirac Hamiltonian on the ring, `H = cpσ_z + Mc²σ_x`, has the two branches `±E` with `E² = p²c² + M²c⁴`, and on the positive branch the mean of `σ_z` is `cos θ = pc/E` (`doublet_branches`, `doublet_mean`, Sec. III).

`Offsets.lean` formalizes the sentence of Sec. IV that the sum of the offsets around a closed ring is what a quantum of exchange changes. Offsets between cells are values in `ZMod L`; the ones that come from a choice of marks (a coboundary) telescope to zero around any closed path (`pathSum_coboundary`, `closed_coboundary`), so the closed sum is the same for every choice of marks (`closed_sum_invariant`); no choice of marks changes one offset alone: marks that leave every other offset around a closed path unchanged leave that one unchanged too (`no_single_offset`); and a quantum added to one pair changes the sum along any path that steps through the pair once, closed paths included, by exactly that quantum (`quantum_changes_closed_sum`). On a ring of `n + 1` cells, two offset assignments with the same total differ by a choice of marks (`ring_offsets`); the modulus `L` of the offsets and the number of cells are independent in the statement, and both are `L` in the register.

`Vertex.lean` formalizes the count of Sec. IV, with every step a theorem and the junctions between the steps checked too. Two definitions carry the paper's reading of the register into Lean: the readings of one leg's momentum in a span of dimension `s` are a magnitude with `L` values and `s − 1` angles with `L` values each (`alphabet`), and the cells of the sphere are `L` values in each of `d − 1` coordinates (`grid`). So a leg has `L^s` momenta (`alphabet_card`) and the sphere has `L^(d−1)` cells (`grid_card`, Eq. (cells)). Postulate 2 puts one cell on each momentum, so the two counts are equal, and `L^s = L^(d−1)` gives `s = d − 1` (`span_of_postulate`). Conserved momenta, `n + 1` of them summing to zero, span at most `n` dimensions (`finrank_span_le_of_sum_zero`), exactly `n` when the first `n` are independent (`finrank_span_eq_of_sum_zero`). `dimension_eq_legs` takes the momenta of a vertex, their sum being zero, the independence of the first `n`, and Postulate 2 as the equality of the two counts with the dimension of the span of the momenta in the alphabet, and concludes `d = n + 1` (Eq. (nk)); the proof computes the span's dimension, turns both counts into powers of `L`, and reads off `n = d − 1`, so nothing is written in by hand. `three` is the case of three legs, `d = 3` (Eq. (three)). Counting the orientation of the plane as well, `2(d − 2)` more angles, would break the count (`orientation_not_counted`). The coincidence of two uncorrelated strings at one cell is one pair in `L` (`coincidence`, Eq. (coincidence)) and, if two rings share two cells, `2/L²` (`coincidence_two_cells`). A law that commutes with the shift preserves the shift's eigenspaces, so the mode number is conserved (`eigenspace_preserved`). The space here is any finite-dimensional real vector space, the linear structure at the point of the vertex that the setting assumes, and nothing more.

`Sphere.lean` formalizes Secs. V–VII. On the two-sphere the fraction of the area within polar angle `θ` of the pole is `u₁ = (1 − cos θ)/2` (`u_two_sphere`, Eq. (u)), so `cos²(θ/2) = 1 − u₁` is Palmer's `m/L` (`cos_sq_half_eq`), and the `L` values of `u₁` cut the sphere into bands of equal area `4πR²/L` (`band_area`). The area of the unit sphere, Eq. (omega), is `2π`, `4π`, `2π²`, `8π²/3` at `d = 2, 3, 4, 5` (`Ω_two` to `Ω_five`, from `Γ(1/2) = √π` and `Γ(x + 1) = xΓ(x)`), the Planck-pixel count of Eq. (supply) is `L`, `L²/π`, `L³/4π`, `L⁴/6π²` (`Npix_two` to `Npix_five`, Table I), and the ratio `C(d) = Ω_{d−1}/(2π)^{d−1}` of Eq. (C) is `1`, `1/π`, `1/4π` (`C_two`, `C_three`, `C_four`). A cell of the grid on the horizon has area `ℓ_P²/π` (`cell_area`, Eq. (cell)), and a Planck pixel subtends one notch, `2π/L` (`pixel_subtends_notch`, Sec. VII).

`Root.lean` formalizes Sec. VI, the relaxed count `Ω_{d−1}(L/2π)^{d−1} = L²` with `d` real, that is `g(d) = 0` with `g(d) = (d − 3) + ln C(d)/ln L`. Everything rests on one fact about `Γ`, that `ln Γ` is convex on `(0, ∞)` (the Bohr–Mollerup theorem, Mathlib's `Real.convexOn_log_Gamma`), together with `Γ(1) = Γ(2) = 1`, `Γ(1/2) = √π` and `Γ(x + 1) = xΓ(x)`. No derivative is taken: where the paper argues with the digamma function, the file argues with the slopes of `ln Γ`. On `[1, 2]`, `−(x − 1) ln π ≤ ln Γ(x) ≤ 0` (`f_ge`, `f_nonpos`), so on `[2, 4]` the ratio `C` decreases and `1/4π ≤ C(d) ≤ 1` (`logC_strictAnti`, `logC_ge`, `logC_nonpos`, Eq. (C)). Then `g(2) = −1` and `g(4) = 1 − ln 4π/ln L > 0` for `L > 4π` (`g_two`, `g_four_eq`, `g_four`), so a root exists in `[2, 4]` (`root_exists`, the intermediate value theorem); any root there satisfies `0 ≤ d − 3 ≤ ln 4π/ln L` (`root_bound`, Eq. (bound)); and `g` is strictly increasing on `(0, L²/2π)` for every `L > 1` (`g_strictMono`), so the root is the only solution below `L²/2π` (`unique_root`). The chords of `ln Γ` at `3/2` give the tighter bracket `ln π/(ln L − ln π) ≤ d − 3 ≤ ln π/(ln L − ln 4)` (`f_near_three_halves`, `logC_near_three`, `g_at_bracket`, `root_bracket`), which is what fixes the root to four decimals. Dividing the count by `c ≥ 1` moves the root up by at least `ln c/ln L` and at most that over `1 − K/ln L`, `K = (3/2) ln 2 + ½ ln π` (`g_slope`, `pixel_shift`). The factor that puts the root at `7/2` is `√L · C(7/2)`, between `√L/2π` and `√L/π^{3/2}`, and the one that puts it at `5/2` is `√L/C(5/2)`, between `√(2L)` and `√(πL)` (`seven_halves_factor`, `five_halves_factor`).

`Numerics.lean` recomputes every number the paper quotes from its inputs, stated as exact rationals, with Mathlib's `π`, `ln 2` and `e` and their bounds, and certifies each by an interval that the quoted value rounds to. The register length is `L = 2πR_Λ/ℓ_P` with `R_Λ = √(3/Λ)` computed as `c/(H₀√Ω_Λ)`, since the paper cites Planck 2018 without numbers; from the constants and the Planck 2018 parameters it lies in `(6.446, 6.447) × 10⁶¹` (`Lval_bounds`; the paper rounds to `6.4 × 10⁶¹` and uses that rounded value, `L₀`, in what follows). Then `L₀² = 4.096 × 10¹²³` (`mom_count`), the pixel counts and ratios of Table I (`Npix_three_val`, `Npix_four_val`, `Npix_five_val`, `ratios_val`), `ℓ_P²/π = 0.318 ℓ_P²` (`inv_pi_val`), the `L₀²/4π = 3.3 × 10¹²²` units of horizon area (`horizon_units`), `2²⁰⁵ < L₀ < 2²⁰⁶` with `2^N ≤ L₀` exactly for `N ≤ 205` (`capacity_205`, `nmax_205`), `log₂ L₀ = 205.3`, `616` bits of position and `409` bits of area (`bits`, `bits_three`, `bits_area`), and `ℏL₀/2 = 3.4 × 10²⁷ J s` (`ang_mom_val`). For Sec. VI it derives `ln L₀ ∈ (142.3138, 142.3142)`, `ln π ∈ (1.1447, 1.1448)` and `ln 10 ∈ (2.302, 2.3032)` from `e^x < 1/(1 − x)` and `1 + x ≤ e^x` raised to a power (`log_L0_bounds`, `log_pi_bounds`, `log_ten_bounds`), and with them certifies the leading term `3 + ln π/ln L₀ = 3.0080` (`leading_term`), the bound `ln 4π/ln L₀ = 0.018` (`bound_value`), the shift `ln 4/ln L₀ = 0.010` (`pixel_choice`), the root itself, `3.00810 < d* < 3.00813`, unique below `L₀²/2π = 6.5 × 10¹²²` (`root_at_L0`, `uniqueness_range`), the roots at `10L₀` and `L₀/10` within `2 × 10⁻⁴` of it (`factor_of_ten`), the shift of the root for pixels of side `2ℓ_P`, between `0.0097` and `0.0099` (`pixel_shift_val`), and the factors that would put the root at `7/2`, in `(1.27, 1.44) × 10³⁰`, and at `5/2`, in `(1.13, 1.42) × 10³¹` (`seven_halves_factor_val`, `five_halves_factor_val`).

`Ledger.lean` prints the axioms of every main theorem.

## The inputs, and where they enter

| Input | In the code | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| Postulate 1: the shift moves every value one cell on, and the ring closes after `L` cells | the definitions `mode`, `root`, `shift` in `Ring.lean` | everything in `Ring.lean` |
| The register's reading of a momentum in a span of dimension `s`: a magnitude with `L` values and `s − 1` angles with `L` values each; and the cells of the sphere: `L` values in each of `d − 1` coordinates | the definitions `alphabet` and `grid` in `Vertex.lean` | `alphabet_card`, `grid_card`, `dimension_eq_legs`, `three` |
| Postulate 2: one cell of the sphere on each momentum of a leg, so the two counts are equal | `hpost` in `dimension_eq_legs` and `three`; `h` in `span_of_postulate` is the same statement with the counts already taken | `dimension_eq_legs`, `three`, `span_of_postulate` |
| Conservation: the momenta of a vertex sum to zero | `hsum` | `finrank_span_le_of_sum_zero`, `finrank_span_eq_of_sum_zero`, `dimension_eq_legs`, `three` |
| The vertex's momenta obey no relation beyond conservation: the first `n` of the `n + 1` are independent, so the three-leg vertex's momenta span a plane | `hind` | `finrank_span_eq_of_sum_zero`, `dimension_eq_legs`, `three` |
| A plane's orientation is `2(d − 2)` angles | `h` in `orientation_not_counted` | `orientation_not_counted` |
| The Dirac Hamiltonian on the ring, `H = cpσ_z + Mc²σ_x` | the matrix entries in `doublet_branches`, `hE2` | `doublet_branches`, `doublet_mean` |
| The constants `c`, `ℏ`, `G` (CODATA 2018) and `H₀ = 67.4 km/s/Mpc`, `Ω_Λ = 0.685` (Planck 2018) | the local notations of `Numerics.lean` | `Lval_bounds`, `ang_mom_val` |
| The paper's rounded `L₀ = 6.4 × 10⁶¹` | the local notation `L₀` | every other number |

Nothing else is assumed. `L > 4π` is the hypothesis of the theorems on the root, since `g(4) > 0` needs it; `L₀` exceeds it by sixty orders of magnitude. What Lean cannot check is that the definitions `alphabet` and `grid` are the right model of the register, or that the constants are the right numbers: a proof checker confirms what follows from a definition, not that the definition matches the world. Everything that follows from them is checked.

## What was checked, and what it found

Every relation of the paper that the files cover holds as written, and every number is certified except one, which the check corrects: each theorem gives an interval every point of which rounds to the value the paper quotes. Four things the check adds to the text.

The equation of Sec. VI has a second root. `g_strictMono` shows that `g` increases on `(0, L²/2π)`, so the root is the only solution below `L²/2π = 6.5 × 10¹²²`, which is what `unique_root` states; the files do not prove that a second root exists. Numerically there is one, near `d = eL²/2π = 1.8 × 10¹²³`, where the growth of `Γ(d/2)` has overtaken `(d − 3) ln L`. The abstract of v16-29 says that the relaxed count has one solution near three and no other below 10¹²², and Sec. VI says that the left side increases until beyond `L²/2π = 6.5 × 10¹²²`, so the root is the only solution below that value; both are what is certified.

The six-digit root `3.008117` is a numerical evaluation. What is certified is the bracket `ln π/(ln L − ln π) ≤ d* − 3 ≤ ln π/(ln L − ln 4)`, which at `L₀` gives `3.00810 < d* < 3.00813`: the root is `3.0081` to four decimals and `3.008` to three, and the leading term `3 + ln π/ln L₀` is `3.0080`. The bound `ln 4π/ln L₀ = 0.018` and the statement that a factor of ten in `L` moves the root by less than `2 × 10⁻⁴` are certified as stated.

The half-integer remark is corrected. Versions up to v16-28 gave `2.5 × 10³⁰` as the factor that would put the root at a half-integer; that number is `√L/π`, which uses `C(3)` where `C(7/2)` belongs. To put the root at `7/2` the count would have to be divided by `√L · C(7/2)`, which at `L₀` lies between `1.27 × 10³⁰` and `1.44 × 10³⁰` (the value is `1.3 × 10³⁰`); to put it at `5/2` the count would have to be multiplied by `√L/C(5/2)`, between `1.13 × 10³¹` and `1.42 × 10³¹` (the value is `1.4 × 10³¹`). Sec. VI of v16-29 gives `c = 1.3 × 10³⁰` for `7/2` and, for `5/2`, a factor of more than `10³¹`.

The register length from the paper's inputs is `6.4467 × 10⁶¹` (`Lval_bounds`), which the paper rounds to `6.4 × 10⁶¹`; every number after that is computed from the rounded value, as in the paper, and `L₀² = 4.096 × 10¹²³` is the exact square of it, the paper's `4.10 × 10¹²³`.

What is not certified, because it is physics, a definition, or a remark rather than a step of the mathematics: that the register's event has three legs and that their momenta are not collinear (Sec. IV), which enter `three` as hypotheses; that two rings in different planes meet in two cells, of which `coincidence_two_cells` checks only the arithmetic `2/L²`; that every record is made with the three-leg vertex; that a plane's orientation is `2(d − 2)` angles, of which `orientation_not_counted` checks only the arithmetic; the value `1/C = 3.1745` at the root; the existence of the second root beyond `L²/2π`; the six-digit value `3.008117`, of which the files certify `3.0081`; the equal-area partition of the sphere for `d > 3`, which the paper takes from the literature and the files check only for the two-sphere; the remark on the digamma function, which the files replace by the slopes of `ln Γ`; Sec. IX, the Clifford reading, which the files do not touch; and the citations of Niven and Clifford, which are citations.
